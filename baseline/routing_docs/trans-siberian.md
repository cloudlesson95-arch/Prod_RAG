# The Trans-Siberian Railway

The Trans-Siberian Railway is the longest railway line in the world. Its main route runs about 9,289 kilometres from Moscow in western Russia to Vladivostok on the Pacific coast. It was built between 1891 and 1916, starting under Tsar Alexander III and finished under Nicholas II.

## The journey

A through train from Moscow to Vladivostok takes about seven days without getting off. On the way it crosses eight time zones, so passengers change their watches almost every day. Near Yekaterinburg, at kilometre 1,777, an obelisk marks the border between Europe and Asia, where the line leaves the Ural Mountains.

## Lake Baikal and the branch lines

East of Irkutsk the railway runs along the southern shore of Lake Baikal, the deepest lake on Earth. Two branch routes leave the main line for China: the Trans-Manchurian route goes through Harbin to Beijing, and the Trans-Mongolian route goes through Ulaanbaatar, the capital of Mongolia, to Beijing.

## Track and electrification

Like all Russian railways, the line uses broad gauge track, 1,520 millimetres between the rails, so trains entering China change their bogies at the border. Electrification of the whole route from Moscow to Vladivostok was completed in 2002.
