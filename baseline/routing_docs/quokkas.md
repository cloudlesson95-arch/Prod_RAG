# Quokkas

The quokka (Setonix brachyurus) is a small marsupial from Western Australia and the only member of the genus Setonix. It belongs to the macropod family, which also includes kangaroos and wallabies. An adult quokka weighs between 2.5 and 5 kilograms and is 40 to 54 centimetres long, with a tail of another 25 to 30 centimetres.

## Where quokkas live

In the wild, quokkas live mainly on two islands off the coast of Western Australia: Rottnest Island, about 19 kilometres west of Perth, and Bald Island, near Albany. Rottnest Island has the largest population, estimated at 10,000 to 12,000 animals. Small, scattered groups also survive on the mainland, in dense vegetation around swamps in the south-west.

Rottnest Island owes its name to the quokka. In 1696 the Dutch explorer Willem de Vlamingh mistook the animals for giant rats and named the island "Rottenest", meaning "rat nest" in Dutch.

## Diet and behaviour

Quokkas are herbivores. They eat grasses, sedges, succulents and the leaves of shrubs, and they can go for long periods without drinking because they get most of their water from plants. They are mostly nocturnal, resting in shady vegetation during the day and feeding at night.

## Breeding

A female quokka gives birth to a single young, called a joey, after a pregnancy of about one month. The joey crawls into its mother's pouch, where it stays for around six months. After leaving the pouch it continues to drink its mother's milk for a few more months.

## Conservation

The quokka is listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. On the mainland, introduced foxes, habitat clearing and bushfires have greatly reduced its numbers. The island populations are safer because foxes never reached them.

## The happiest animal in the world

Quokkas are often called "the happiest animal in the world" because the shape of their mouth makes them look as if they are smiling. Visitors to Rottnest Island take so-called quokka selfies with them. Touching or feeding quokkas is illegal on Rottnest Island and can lead to a fine, because human food makes them sick.
